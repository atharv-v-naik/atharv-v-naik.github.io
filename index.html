Kernel

Where I do my research, and where most people stop looking.

Extending AutoPerf++

M.Tech Project I, July 2026 to now. SynerG Lab, IIT Bombay, guided by Prof. Varsha Apte.

AutoPerf++ finds performance bottlenecks in networked applications automatically, without an expert reading profiles for days.

I am building its kernel profiling engine and a new subsystem that discovers an application's TCP sockets on its own, starting from a single port. Both run parallel strace workers so that finding a bottleneck becomes a command, not a project.

The best way to show what that means is an example. Here is one real case it cracked open.

  1. Build a real testbed

    I moved the evaluation from a simple single-tier app to the TechEmpower Fortunes benchmark: Nginx in front of Gunicorn in front of PostgreSQL, on a large many-core server with a separate load generator.

  2. Hit a strange ceiling

    AutoPerf++'s automatic load search, which I had made reliable at high throughput, found a hard limit. Requests piled up, yet most of the server's cores were doing nothing.

  3. Listen to the kernel

    I traced system calls on every tier, found automatically by following socket connections from port 80, and compared each process's calm and loaded behaviour with TF-IDF and cosine similarity. Gunicorn's pattern changed dramatically. PostgreSQL's barely moved.

  4. Do the maths

    Gunicorn was running a single synchronous worker. Each worker opens its own pool of database connections, so I worked out the largest worker count PostgreSQL's connection limit could take without refusing clients.

  5. Prove it

    Scaling to that worker count woke the idle cores, multiplied capacity, and pulled Gunicorn's system calls back towards normal. I wrote playbooks so anyone can reproduce the fix or revert it.

Faster load testing

M.Tech R&D Project II, January to May 2026

Load tests in AutoPerf++ kept repeating work and stalling at high user counts. I added a concurrency-aware result cache that skips duplicate runs, which made tests converge about three times faster, and replaced a fixed stopping rule with a relative tolerance so they stop stalling.

Python, Taurus, closed-loop load generation

Survey of the field

M.Tech Seminar, January to May 2026

I compared the published tools for finding scalability bottlenecks, including Speedup Stacks, ScaAnalyzer and Whodunnit, and traced why multi-core software stops scaling: memory bus saturation, cache interference between cores, and undersized thread pools.

System-call traces, hardware performance counters

Bigool

Android app and web platform, 2023. Live on Google Play.

Companies with staff out in the field needed to know where their people were, confirm attendance, and pay travel claims without paperwork.

I built the Android app and its backend. The app tracks location in the background without draining the battery, sampling more often when you move and less when you stand still. Attendance is a selfie checked against the site's geofence, so nobody can mark in for a friend. When the signal drops, everything is buffered on the phone and synced the moment the network comes back. Distance travelled is calculated from the GPS trail with jitter filtered out, which turns mileage claims into a report instead of an argument.

Managers watch it live and replay past routes on the web dashboard at bigool.in.

Java, Android foreground services, Fused Location, SQLite, PHP, MySQL, Leaflet
